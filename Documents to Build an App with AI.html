The golden rule: AI drafts, humans decide.

AI is excellent at first drafts, boilerplate, test scaffolding and formatting. It is unreliable at inventing requirements you never stated, choosing trade-offs with legal or safety consequences, inventing libraries/APIs, and judging whether its own output is correct. Every AI-generated document or code change needs a named human owner, a review gate, and — for code — automated tests/scans before merge.

How to read this page: E = Essential R = Recommended O = Optional   Owner: H = human owns, AI = AI can draft, H+AI = AI drafts, human reviews and signs. Legal, security, safety, privacy, pricing and release decisions always need human approval — even when AI drafted the text. * in the table = essential if the app uses AI / RAG.

What AI can draft vs. what a human must own

✓ AI can draft — human reviews

  • PRD first draft, user stories, test cases
  • API boilerplate / OpenAPI skeletons
  • Wireframe variations, UI copy
  • README, changelog, help docs
  • Code and unit tests
  • Risk-register candidates
  • Competitor matrix draft

⚑ Human must write, approve or own

  • Problem statement, MVP scope + Won’t-have list, pricing / monetization
  • Architecture decisions (ADRs), model + prompt choices, evaluation thresholds
  • Guardrail / refusal policy, security requirements + threat model, auth design
  • Privacy policy, terms, consent flows, compliance classification
  • UAT sign-off, release go / no-go, incident & breach decisions
  • Any high-stakes AI output policy — health, legal, finance, safety

Templates & standards to start from

Starting points from the research (§14) — not legal advice. Privacy policies, terms, DPAs and high-risk AI assessments should be reviewed by a qualified lawyer in the relevant jurisdiction.

  • Smartsheet PRD templates — PRD starting point (verified live 2026-10-28 in the research)
  • Google Play AI-generated content policy (official) — prevent restricted content, in-app reporting/flagging (verified live)
  • Store listing preparation — sizes & limits (verified live)
  • Swagger Editor — OpenAPI auth guide
  • OWASP ASVS — security requirements baseline
  • Apple App Store Review Guidelines — the research notes the primary source is developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines and it was not directly opened in this research; verify the live version before submission.

Cautions carried from the research — read before you quote numbers: Vendor statistics (discovery ROI, scope-creep reduction, a secondary claim that ~45% of AI-generated code samples contained an OWASP Top 10 flaw, slopsquatting rates) come from vendor/agency pages and were not independently verified — directionally useful, not facts to quote to stakeholders. Store fees ($99/yr Apple, $25 one-off Google Play) and icon/screenshot sizes are Oct 2026 secondary-source figures — confirm in App Store Connect / Play Console. EU AI Act high-risk/transparency dates in 2026–2028 are reported as shifting — verify the official timeline at build time. DPDP figures (penalties up to ₹250 crore, Rules dates) should be verified against the official Gazette / MeitY notifications. WCAG sources cite both 2.1 AA and 2.2 AA — state one explicitly in your accessibility document.

Could not verify: the live text of Apple’s current App Review Guidelines and Google’s Data Safety form field-by-field; primary Veracode 2025 figures; primary DPDP Rules text/dates; primary EU AI Act consolidated timeline as of Oct 2026. No project-specific parameters were supplied (app type, geography, regulated domain, team size, budget) — so the E/R/O ratings here are generic. A health, finance or children’s app moves many “Recommended” items to “Essential” and adds sector rules not detailed here.
